Ifa Cube is the cubing platform of the IFA Internet: the space where cube structures, cube laws and three-dimensional systems are modelled as Odu configurations. Where the Ifa Square gives one Odu two arms (length and breadth), the Ifa Cube gives one Odu three arms (length, breadth and height). Ẹ̀ta means three, and three arms of two-state marks give the cube its 2³ = 8 corners.

Alternative names: Ifa Cube is also written as IfaCube, Ifa-Cube, Consciousness Cube, Energy-Based Cube, the Cube for Everything (CuboE), Ifa Hypercube and Odu Hypercube.

The Power of Three

Why 2³: Ẹ̀ta and the Cube

Every Odu mark has two states, single or double. A square has two arms, so it grows as 2²; a cube has three, so it grows as 2³. Ẹ̀ta is the exponent.

Beyond Three Arms · 2ⁿ → ∞

From IfaPoint to the Duoinfinicube

The IfaSquare and the IfaCube are two steps on one ladder. Each new arm doubles the corners, so n arms give 2ⁿ corners and 3ⁿ cells in all. Let the arms go on without end and the ladder reaches the Infinity Cube; let the length of every arm go on without end as well and it reaches the Duoinfinity Cube.

The Infinity Cube · Infinicube

Give the cube one arm for every counting number. Each corner is then an endless string of single and double marks, an Odu that never ends. There are as many corners as there are real numbers; mathematicians know this space as the Cantor space. Every finite Ifa cube sits inside it, the way an IfaSquare sits on each face of the IfaCube.

∞∞

The Duoinfinity Cube · Duoinfinicube · Ifa Infinity Cube

Infinite in two ways at once: the number of arms has no end, and the length of every arm has no end. This is the cube counterpart of the doubly-infinite Ifa Square, which holds n×n squares of every size and type. With k positions on each of n arms the cube holds kⁿ points and (2k−1)ⁿ cells; the Odu cubes are the row k = 2.

One Odu at a point, on a line, a square, a cube, a hypercube, and onward without end: 2⁰ · 2¹ · 2² · 2³ · 2⁴ · … · 2^∞ · ∞^∞.

Coined term

Duoinfinity Cube (Duoinfinicube), also called the Ifa Infinity Cube: coined by CENProject / The IFA Internet.

Key references

The hypercube and the infinite cube

  1. “Hypercube”, Wikipedia. The n-dimensional analogue of the square and the cube: 2ⁿ vertices, and 2n−m·C(n,m) faces of dimension m; the 4-cube (tesseract) has 8 cubic cells.
  2. E. W. Weisstein, “Hypercube Graph”, Wolfram MathWorld. Vertices are the binary strings of length n, joined when they differ in exactly one place — the same rule as Odu that differ by one mark.
  3. H. S. M. Coxeter, Regular Polytopes, 3rd ed., Dover, 1973. The standard account of the measure polytopes (n-cubes) in every dimension.
  4. “Hilbert cube”, Wikipedia; see also Encyclopedia of Mathematics. The cube of countably infinite dimension: the product of countably many intervals.
  5. “Cantor space”, Wikipedia. The space 2ω of infinite binary sequences, with the cardinality of the continuum — the corners of the Infinicube.

Ifá, binary codes and the Odù

  1. A. S. Olagunju, A. A. James, E. O. Adeyefa and F. L. Joseph, “Algebraic characterization of Ifa main divination codes”, Scientific African 20 (2023) e01729. Writes the 16 principal Odu as binary codes and shows they form an abelian group under addition mod 2 — the symmetry group of the Odu Hypercube.
  2. F. O. Alamu, H. O. Aworinde and W. I. Isharufe, “A Comparative Study of Ifa Divination and Computer Science”, International Journal of Innovative Technology and Research 1(6) (2013) 524–528. Reads the eight two-state seeds of the ọ̀pẹ̀lẹ̀ as a binary code for the 256 Odu.
  3. R. Eglash, “Africa in the Origins of the Binary Code”, in Digital Hyperstition, Urbanomic. On African divination traditions and the history of binary code.
  4. W. Coleman, O. Daniel and B. Ost, chart of the 256 Odu, African Traditional Religions: Ifa — Hermeneutics, Atla LibGuides. A geometrical arrangement of the 256 Odu (the source of the Ifalibrium II chart).
  5. “Odu Ifa”, Wikipedia. Overview of the 16 principal Odu and the 256 Odu Ifa.
