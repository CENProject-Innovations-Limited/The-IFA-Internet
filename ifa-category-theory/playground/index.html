Gbàgéde Àpólà Odù: Ifa Category Theory Playground

Gbàgéde playground · Àpólà IfaCategory (Ifa Category) · Odù the Odù Ifá

Odù Ifá has a natural higher-categorical Structure, and it comes straight from how the marks work and interact based on the 16 Principles/Axioms of Symmetry, Invariance, Duality, Composition, and others. Explore it here, one level at a time: the 16 principal Odù as objects, the 256 Odù Ifá as arrows, symmetries as functors, changes of convention as natural transformations, and the hypercube’s cells as the higher levels.

The ladder of higher categories, and Ifá’s own

Category theory adds one level of “relationship between relationships” at each step. Ifá supplies every level from its marks alone.

Two ideas make the ∞ case work

Homotopy coherence. Two composites need not be equal. They only need to be joined by a higher cell, and those joins are themselves joined one level up, and so on.

The working model. An (∞,1)-category, as Joyal and Lurie developed it (quasi-categories), has objects and morphisms at level 1, and every cell above level 1 is invertible. The standard way to build one is the nerve: chains of composable arrows become simplices (points, edges, triangles, tetrahedra, …).

Ifá’s ladder: the Odù hypercube

Writing 1 for a single mark and 0 for a double mark, four marks to a leg, the 16 principal Odù are the corners of a four-dimensional cube. Its cells form a genuine ladder of levels.

16level 0 · corners · the 16 principal Odù
32level 1 · edges · change exactly one mark
24level 2 · squares · two changes in either order agree
8level 3 · cubes · three changes in every order
1level 4 · the whole Odù space

The counts add up to 81 = 3⁴. For the eight-mark Odù Ifá the same construction gives the 8-cube: 256 corners and 6,561 = 3⁸ cells in all. So Ifá’s ladder stops at a depth equal to the number of marks: 4 for one leg, 8 for a full Odù Ifá. The principal Odù form a 4-dimensional cubical structure, and the full Odù Ifá an 8-dimensional one.

A proposed structure for Ifa Category Theory

  1. Ifá 0-category: the 16 principal Odù and the 256 Odù Ifá as objects.
  2. Ifá 1-category: the Amulu category (connection) and the lattice of becoming (direction).
  3. Ifá 2-category: the symmetries (reversal, complement) as functors, and changes of convention as natural transformations.
  4. Ifá n-category: the hypercube’s cells up to dimension 4 (8 for Odù Ifá), with the permutohedron as the space of paths.
  5. Ifá functors outward: logic, computing (a 4-bit nibble, an 8-bit byte), and any field shown to share the structure.

An honest boundary: the mathematics on this page is exact and checked live in each tab, while readings such as “becoming” and “connection” are interpretations. Keeping that line visible makes the programme stronger with mathematicians.

The Amulu category: 256 Odù Ifá as arrows

Take the 16 principal Odù as objects and the compound Odù A-B as the one arrow from A to B. Composition is (A-B) then (B-C) = A-C. This is the indiscrete category on 16 objects, with exactly 16 × 16 = 256 arrows, the 256 Odù Ifá. The Méjì on the diagonal are the identity arrows.

One caution: every object is joined to every other in exactly one way, so this category is “contractible” (equivalent to a single point). It captures connection, not difference; the lattice in the Hypercube tab supplies the difference.

Paths, paths between paths, and beyond: the permutohedron

There are exactly 4! = 24 paths from Ọ̀yẹ̀kú (all double) to Ogbè (all single), one for each order of turning the four marks single. Two paths are joined by a 2-cell when they differ only by swapping two consecutive changes, which goes round one square of the cube. The 24 paths and their swaps form the permutohedron: 24 corners, 36 edges, 14 faces (8 hexagons and 6 squares). Its faces are the level-3 coherences; the solid itself is the top cell.

Functors: the Major pairs are a symmetry

Two structure-preserving maps act on the Odù. Complement (N) swaps single and double marks and turns the lattice upside down (rank k ↦ 4 − k). Reversal (R) reads the leg upside down and keeps each rank. The traditional Major pairs follow one rule: each Odù’s partner is its reversal, except the four that read the same both ways (Ogbè, Ọ̀yẹ̀kú, Ìwòrì, Òdí), whose partner is their complement.

Natural transformations: changes that work uniformly

Between two order-preserving maps F and G on the lattice, a natural transformation F ⇒ G exists exactly when F(A) ≤ G(A) for every Odù A, and then there is only one. In plain terms, a natural transformation is a change of convention that works uniformly, which gives a rigorous way to say which differences between notations are merely conventional.

Example 2 · two ways of writing the Odù

One convention writes a leg top to bottom; another writes it bottom to top. These are two functors from the Odù to written codes, and reversal turns one into the other in the same way for every Odù: a natural isomorphism.

A functor outward: the 16 Odù are the 16 logic gates

Read an Odù’s four marks as the outputs for the inputs (yes,yes), (yes,no), (no,yes), (no,no). The 16 principal Odù then match the 16 logical operations on two inputs one to one, and the order carries over too. Complement becomes NOT, and the Major pairing becomes “negate both inputs”, so Ọ̀bàrà (AND) partners Ọ̀kànràn (NOR), as logic predicts.
