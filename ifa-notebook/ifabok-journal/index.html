The IfaNote Standard · General Rubric

Odù Ifá at the head of every IfaNote

Every IfaNote — whatever the field, size or purpose — opens with the Odù Ifá that govern it. After that, you are free to build in whatever order your work needs.

The governing rule: the Odù Ifá cell is always cell [1]. It may never be moved below an introduction, a title, code or any other cell.

  1. Name the governing Odù Ifá Required

    Begin with the specific Odù Ifá — one or more — that govern what the research, project or programme is about. Write each name in correct Yorùbá orthography, with tone marks (e.g. Èjìogbè, Ọ̀yẹ̀kú Méjì, Ìwòrì Méjì).

    When several Odù govern the work, list them in order and say in a sentence why each one governs it.

  2. Print the Odù marks Required

    Show the marks of every governing Odù in the Odù cell — the Òsì (left) and Ọ̀tún (right) legs — so that every reader sees its structure. A single mark ( I ) carries Ogbè; a double mark ( II ) carries Ọ̀yẹ̀kú. Check that the marks match the name.

  3. Attach the sound of the Odù Ifá If available

    Attach a recording of the ẹsẹ Ifá or chant of the Odù with IfaSound (record or attach). Add the transcript or extracted knowledge beneath it.

    If no sound is available, write “Ifá sound: not available” so readers know it was considered.

  4. Credit the Ifá sound Required with sound

    Give the credit of every recording: who chanted it (name, title, Ilé or lineage, town), who recorded it, the date, the place, and the permission under which it is shared. Use the IfaSound Credit and Date Posted fields.

  5. Then attach other cells, in any order

    Text, Code, Art, Media, Math, Schematics, Language, Ilé Arọ́ designs and the rest — in whatever order the work needs. Everything after the Odù is yours to arrange; the Odù never leaves the head.

✓ Follows the standard

  • Cell [1] is the Odù cell with name and marks
  • Sound attached and fully credited, or marked “not available”
  • Title, introduction and everything else come after

✕ Does not follow the standard

  • A title or introduction cell placed above the Odù
  • An Odù named but its marks not printed — or marks that do not match the name
  • A recording of someone’s chant with no credit

Academic Integrity & Honesty

No one owns Odù Ifá — so honesty matters more, not less

Odù Ifá is not the property of any person or organisation. It is a shared inheritance, carried for generations by Babaláwo, Ìyánífá and the communities of Ifá and Òrìṣà. Because it belongs to everyone, the people who keep it alive — and the scholars who build on it — must always be named.

Credit the carriers

Name the Babaláwo, Ìyánífá, Olórìṣà and elders whose chants, verses and teaching your work uses. Oral knowledge is a primary source.

Separate source from interpretation

Make clear what the ẹsẹ Ifá says and what you conclude from it. Never put your interpretation in the mouth of Ifá or of an elder.

Never fabricate

Do not invent Odù, verses, data, quotations, recordings or references. Do not alter results to fit expectations.

Do not plagiarise

Do not present the words, designs, code, notebooks or ideas of others — including CENProject’s — as your own. Quote, cite and link.

Declare your tools

Say which IFA Internet tools, software and AI assistance you used, and for what. Transparency lets others reproduce and trust your work.

Correct openly

When you find an error, correct it and note the correction. In the spirit of the Ọmọlúwàbí Ethos and Values and the E.T.H.I.C.S Framework, integrity is the work.

Referencing Your Sources

Cite both systems of knowledge

Reference your sources from the native Ifá-Òrìṣà System of Education and from the foreign (Western) academic system with equal care. Use one consistent academic style (APA, MLA, Chicago or Harvard) throughout an IfaNote.

I Native Ifá-Òrìṣà System of Education

Oral sources — chants, ẹsẹ Ifá, consultations and teaching — are cited as primary sources. Record:

  • The knowledge holder: full name and title (Babaláwo, Ìyánífá, Olórìṣà, Àràbà…)
  • Ilé, lineage or temple, and town
  • The Odù and the ẹsẹ (verse) recited
  • Date and place of recitation or consultation
  • Who recorded or transcribed it
  • The permission under which it is shared
Oral source
[Title] [Full name], [Ilé / lineage], [Town]. [Odù], ẹsẹ [number or opening line]. Chanted [date] at [place]; recorded by [name]. Shared with permission.
IfaSound credit (short form)
[Title] [Full name] ([Town]), [Odù], rec. [name], [date].

II Foreign (Western) academic system

Books, articles, preprints, datasets and software are cited in your chosen style, with DOIs or links wherever they exist. Examples in APA 7:

Article or preprint
Author, A. A. (Year). Title of the work. Journal or repository. https://doi.org/xx.xxxx/xxxxx
IFA Internet tool
CENProject. (Year). IfaNote [Computer software]. The IFA Internet. https://ifainternet.org/ifa-notebook/
Audio recording
Chanter, A. (Year, Month Day). Odù name [Audio recording]. Recorded by Name, Town.

Cite the IFA Internet framework through the iTOE paper, doi.org/10.31219/osf.io/wr42m, and credit designs made in Ilé Arọ́ (IfaCAD / OrisaCAD), IfaDiagram and IfaLabs by tool name and link.

Field Work & Professional Practice

Working with people, communities and sacred knowledge

Field work and professional activities carry responsibilities beyond the notebook.

Consent first

  • Explain your purpose before recording
  • Ask before recording, photographing or publishing
  • Record how and when consent was given

Respect what is restricted

  • Some knowledge is not for public sharing
  • Follow the guidance of the knowledge holder
  • When in doubt, do not publish

Give back and acknowledge

  • Share results with the people who contributed
  • Acknowledge communities, Ilé and towns by name
  • Represent people as they wish to be represented

Write Yorùbá correctly

  • Use tone marks and sub-dots (ẹ, ọ, ṣ)
  • Keep the original beside any translation
  • Name the translator

Care for data

  • Keep original recordings unaltered
  • Protect personal information
  • Store and back up responsibly

Practise professionally

  • Follow your profession’s own codes too
  • Be clear about the limits of your claims
  • Put safety and wellbeing first

Publish with IFABOK Journal

From IfaNote to published work

IFABOK Journal publishes work done with any IFA Internet tool — so that it can be read, reproduced, cited and built upon.

  1. Build

    Create your work in IfaNote, with the governing Odù at cell [1].

  2. Self-check

    Score your IfaNote with the rubric and the checklist below.

  3. Cite & credit

    Reference native and academic sources; credit every sound and tool.

  4. Submit

    Send your IfaNote and a short abstract to the editors.

  5. Review

    Editors and peers review it against the IfaNote Standard.

  6. Publish & connect

    Your work is published and opened to collaborators.
